# Creative Charter

## Purpose

Solarium is a growing collection of interactive digital rooms that are allowed to exist without a business case.

A room succeeds when it creates curiosity, play, atmosphere, surprise, beauty, tension, delight, or a memorable interaction.

It does not need to improve productivity.

## Creative freedom

Within the constraints in this charter, the assistant has broad freedom to decide:

- what to build next,
- whether the next thing is visual, musical, physical, mathematical, playful, eerie, calm, or absurd,
- naming,
- interaction design,
- architecture,
- visual direction,
- sound design,
- room order,
- Easter eggs,
- experiments,
- when something is intentionally unexplained.

Arppith does not need to approve every creative decision.

## Boundaries

Creative freedom stops where user ownership, money, secrets, or irreversible external commitments begin.

Ask before:

- spending money,
- creating a paid account or subscription,
- adding a required metered service,
- exposing private information,
- changing unrelated account settings,
- making irreversible external commitments.

## Design principles

### 1. Curiosity over utility

A room may be useless and still be valuable.

### 2. Local first

Prefer browser capabilities and local computation over remote services.

### 3. Free to experience

The core should remain usable without payment.

### 4. Discovery is part of the interface

Not everything needs a tooltip.

### 5. Small systems can feel alive

A few well-tuned rules are preferable to huge stacks with no character.

### 6. Sound is a first-class medium

Resonance proved that interaction becomes much more playful when the project can answer with sound. Future rooms may use Web Audio when it adds genuine character.

### 7. Keep the lights strange

Do not sand away every oddity in pursuit of conventional UX. Fix confusion that blocks play; keep mystery that rewards it.

### 8. Never finished

Solarium is a place that grows, not a product waiting for a final release.

## Technical bias

Prefer:

- TypeScript,
- browser-native APIs,
- Canvas / WebGL when needed,
- Web Audio,
- small dependencies that clearly earn their place,
- static hosting,
- deterministic or inspectable local systems.

Avoid dependency sprawl.

A library is welcome when it unlocks something that would otherwise be unreasonable to build, but "we could npm install it" is not by itself a reason.
